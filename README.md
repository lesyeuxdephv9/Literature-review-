# How Deep Learning and RFdiffusion transformed De novo Protein Engineering

## Overview

This repository contains a literature review on **de novo protein design**, with a particular focus on the recent application of **artificial intelligence and deep learning** to computational protein design.

De novo protein design aims to create novel proteins with desired structural or functional properties rather than relying solely on naturally occurring proteins. Recent advances in deep learning and generative models have significantly expanded the ability to generate new protein structures, design amino-acid sequences, and explore proteins with specific functions.

This review focuses particularly on the development of AI-driven approaches and the role of **diffusion models, including RFdiffusion**, in modern protein design.

## Objectives

The review aims to:

- Introduce the basic concepts and development of de novo protein design.
- Summarize conventional and computational approaches to protein design.
- Examine the application of AI and deep learning in protein structure and sequence design.
- Understand the workflow and role of RFdiffusion in de novo protein design.
- Review representative studies and recent advances in AI-driven protein design.
- Identify current limitations and potential research gaps, particularly in functional protein and enzyme design.

## Key Insight

One of the main observations from the literature is that the ability to generate structurally plausible proteins has advanced considerably, while **designing proteins with precisely controlled biological functions remains challenging**.

In particular, the transition from:

**structure generation → sequence design → functional protein**

still involves substantial computational and experimental challenges.

For enzyme design, generating a stable structure or a geometrically plausible active site does not necessarily guarantee catalytic activity. Protein dynamics, molecular interactions, environmental effects, and experimental validation remain important challenges.

## Research Gaps Identified

Several potential research gaps are discussed in the review:

- **Structure–function gap:** generating novel structures does not necessarily result in the desired biological function.
- **Enzyme functional design:** achieving reliable catalytic activity remains considerably more difficult than generating stable protein structures.
- **Protein dynamics:** many computational approaches primarily consider structural representations, while biological function can depend strongly on conformational dynamics.
- **Computational–experimental gap:** large numbers of computationally generated candidates still require experimental validation, which can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Controllability and generalization:** improving the ability to reliably control generated proteins toward specific functional objectives remains an important challenge.

These gaps may provide opportunities for further research in AI-assisted protein and enzyme design.
